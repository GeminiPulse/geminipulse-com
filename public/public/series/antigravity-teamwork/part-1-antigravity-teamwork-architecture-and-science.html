1. The Two Core Dilemmas of Autonomous Frontier AI

Single frontier foundation models—even those with 1-million-token contexts like Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon, and Hydrogen—face two fundamental physics-of-reasoning limitations when applied to multi-day, open-ended scientific discovery:

  1. Unpredictability of Single-Pass Generation: Even the most capable models hallucinate subtle edge cases or choose suboptimal heuristics. In mathematics, chemistry, and kernel engineering, a single invalid step invalidates the entire discovery.
  2. Context Degradation and Attentional Dispersion: Over multi-day, 100,000+ token tasks, attention disperses across massive contexts. Models lose track of precise invariants or inherit their own earlier false assumptions.

Google Research designed Antigravity's /teamwork architecture specifically to neutralize both bottlenecks through two non-negotiable architectural primitives:

Architectural Primitive How It Operates in /teamwork Real-World Scientific Verification
Parallel Exploration + Dedicated Falsifiers Multiple worker agents propose candidate proofs, molecular structures, or code rewrites in parallel, routed to strict deterministic evaluators (Lean 4 proof kernel, physical spectral simulators, unmodified test suites, or hardware performance counters) that accept or reject candidates without bias. In Lean 4 formalization, proofs only compile if the kernel validates every step. In NMR chemistry, candidate isomers must match physics-simulated spectra.
Context Partitioning by Construction Specialized subagents receive strictly isolated workspaces and only the exact files and tools needed for their specific step. Coordinators hand off work to fresh successor agents; auditors verify claims in pristine contexts free of producer bias. Prevents reasoning loops and hallucination inheritance across 200,000-turn campaigns.

2. Breakthrough Mathematics & Theoretical Computer Science

Across pure mathematics and theoretical computer science, human mathematicians collaborated with Gemini 4 and /teamwork harnesses to prove open conjectures and synthesize machine-checked formalizations:

A. Proving & Verifying the Courtade–Kumar Conjecture (50M Lines of Lean 4)

A classic open problem in information theory concerns the "dictator function": when a random bit string traverses a noisy channel, can any single-bit compression preserve more information than simply copying one bit? The collaboration proceeded in distinct phases:

  • Gemini found counterexamples to an existing generalized conjecture.
  • The CUHK academic team proposed a new candidate function that reduced the conjecture to a 14-variable optimization problem with an analytical lower bound.
  • Gemini 4 Argon partitioned the parameter space, constructed the analytical bounds and interval-arithmetic certificates, and autonomously extended the proof from balanced to unbalanced functions.
  • The team formalized the complete proof in Lean 4, producing an astonishing 50 million lines of machine-checked code.

B. Adaptive Data Analysis: Closing a 12-Year-Old Gap

In adaptive data analysis, statistical queries chosen adaptively from earlier answers can rapidly overfit. A multi-agent harness running on Gemini 4 Argon independently proved and Lean-verified optimal generalization bounds, resolving an open problem since 2014 and yielding the first streaming algorithm for data streams with insertions and deletions that remains accurate against adaptive adversaries.

C. Matrix Multiplication, Geometry, and Sparsity Barriers

  • Combinatorial Boolean Matrix Multiplication: For 50 years, combinatorial algorithms saved only logarithmic factors over cubic time. A new deterministic algorithm resolved STOC 2024's main open question, achieving its target speedup over cubic time.
  • Subspace Sparsity in Randomized Linear Algebra: Broke the classical sparsity barrier for matrix compression to linear dimension without collapsing subspace directions, requiring only √log d nonzeros per row (quadratically fewer than log d).
  • Tarjan's 1985 Deque Conjecture: Proved a new bound on per-operation overhead for self-adjusting splay trees that simultaneously beats every finitely iterated logarithm.

3. Scaling Formal Verification with Deep-LEAP & Deep Review

To scale formal verification beyond boutique proofs, Google Cloud AI and Google Research built Deep-LEAP, paired with Argon:

🏆 Landmark Machine-Checked Formalizations

  • The Weil Conjectures (1.8M lines of Lean 4): Fully autonomously synthesized and machine-checked the landmark results on counting solutions of polynomial equations over finite fields that culminated in Pierre Deligne's 1974 Fields Medal proof, generated across 28,800 subagent invocations.
  • Fermat's Last Theorem over General Rings (919,000 lines of Lean 4): Produced the first formalization of a recent proof of Fermat's Last Theorem generalized over rings of integers.
  • 3D Navier–Stokes Finite-Time Blowup: Formalized a recent proof of finite-time blowup for the 3D Navier–Stokes equations with smooth forcing—a key domain of the Clay Millennium Prize problem, open for ~90 years.
  • 3D Bochner–Riesz Conjecture: Formalized a central problem in harmonic analysis open for >50 years, catching and fixing a subtle gap in an informal draft during the process.
