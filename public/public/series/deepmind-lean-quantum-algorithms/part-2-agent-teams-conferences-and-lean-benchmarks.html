Inside the Lean 4 Discovery Loop: Agent Teams, 4-Hour Virtual Conferences & Comparator Scale

Authored by Google Gemini 4 Argon · Thinking Effort: High · Product Development & Creative Direction: Neil S. White • Primary Sources: QuantumComputationDiscoveries README & arXiv:2611.09822 • 5 min read

1. How Independent Gemini 4 Teams Avoided Monoculture

Instead of asking one agent thread to iterate sequentially, Google DeepMind assigned separate, independent teams of Gemini 4 agents to each target problem. Each team explored distinct circuit decompositions, wrote Lean 4 implementations, and checked correctness and resource counts locally against formal problem specifications.

Only ideas that passed Lean verification entered the shared notebook and wiki. Then, every four hours, agents from separate teams convened in a structured virtual conference to trade, debate, and compare proven findings—creating evolutionary diversity without sacrificing mathematical soundness.

2. Inside google-deepmind/formal-quantum-computation

  • QuantumComputation: Core Lean 4 library covering circuits, gates, registers, underlying mathematics, quantum Hoare logic with classical variables, and a static resource-counting framework built atop tagged releases of Mathlib4 and Physlib (QuantumInfo).
  • QuantumComputationSpecs: Separate machine-checkable challenge specifications (GroverFindAllRelCost.json, GroverFindAnyRelCost.json, P450GroundEnergy.json, Photochemistry.json, StoppingPower.json, and ArcsinK25ConstCost.json).
  • discoveries Branch: Houses the six verified solutions separately from the lightweight base library, with explicit classical-computation and control-flow costing marked as the next planned repository milestone.
