⚗️ The Principle of Conformational Rigidification:

When a flexible small molecule binds to a protein pocket, it loses rotational freedom across its rotatable bonds. This loss of conformational freedom imposes a thermodynamic entropic penalty (ΔS < 0) that lowers net binding free energy (ΔG). By cyclizing the molecule into a rigid macrocycle before it reaches the pocket, medicinal chemists "pay the entropic cost in advance"—sharply boosting target affinity and selectivity.

1. The Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Case: Crizotinib to Lorlatinib

In Crizotinib, 5 single bonds allow the molecule to adopt hundreds of inactive solution conformations. Lorlatinib closes the terminal ring into a 12-membered macrocycle. The Antigravity agent demonstrated that closing this ring not only eliminates the rotatable bond penalty, but also significantly enhances predicted penetration across the blood-brain barrier—a critical therapeutic requirement for secondary brain metastases.

Because MMPA requires strictly identical contexts around the changing core, Antigravity’s RDKit-grounded skill ensures that pairs differing by confounding functional group modifications are excluded from the causal estimate.

3. Why Random Train/Test Splits Fail in Chemistry ML

A notorious pitfall in computational drug discovery is the "random split illusion." When machine learning models are trained on randomly split chemical databases, compounds with nearly identical core scaffolds appear in both training and test sets. The model simply memorizes the scaffold, achieving misleadingly high R² scores (>0.90) that plummet to zero when tested against novel scaffolds in the wet lab.

The Antigravity agent enforced rigorous data hygiene:

  • Chronological Splits: Training on older published patents/papers (pre-2022) to predict the potency of recently discovered analogs (post-2024), simulating real prospective discovery.
  • Bemis-Murcko Scaffold Splits: Ensuring that no molecular scaffold in the test set shares a ring architecture with the training set, giving an unvarnished estimate of out-of-distribution generalizability.

4. De Novo Macrocycle Design with Retrosynthetic Verification

Finally, Antigravity did not stop at predictive scoring. It generated novel macrocyclic candidates and paired each design with:

  1. Atom-mapped reaction transforms: Verifying that each intermediate can be synthesized using known organic reactions (such as Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling followed by intramolecular macrolactamization).
  2. Electron-pushing arrow diagrams: Graphically illustrating the curly-arrow mechanistic steps for the cyclization stage so bench chemists can immediately assess feasibility.
  3. Predicted target potency: Re-scoring candidate designs against the validated scaffold-split model.
